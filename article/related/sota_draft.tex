\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% Condensed by about a quarter on 2026-11-05 (Pedro: 19 pages); every citation kept.

Automated grading is a long-standing concern of the learning-analytics and educational-data-mining
communities~\cite{putnikovic_embeddings_2023,ferreira_mello_automatic_2025}, where it is valued as an assessment
tool that must be reliable, transparent, and fair before it is used at scale for feedback or for grades that
count~\cite{arz_von_straussenburg_enabling_2025,li_am_2023}. Reliability has several faces (agreement with
teachers, scoring error, and run-to-run consistency), and a strong result on one does not guarantee the others.

\subsection{LLMs as Graders}
Prompted LLMs now match or approach human raters on well-specified short-answer and essay
tasks~\cite{aminah_evaluating_2025,sessler_canai_2025,henkel_roars_2025}, but their agreement depends on the model
and the prompt, and their consistency varies sharply: four LLMs that graded one program seeded with errors,
repeatedly and with one rubric, ranged from 0.29 to 0.83 in intraclass correlation (ICC), the standard index of
agreement between repeated measurements~\cite{moazzez_assessing_2024}. Repeated scoring also drifts over time,
and the variance across runs should be reported with the score~\cite{pack_large_2024}; minimally prompted
grading can stay within one rubric point of teachers yet show low exact agreement with
them~\cite{tate_can_2024}. The same run-to-run unreliability appears when LLMs write code for engineering and
scientific tasks: only a few models produced correct code in every run~\cite{fachada_gpt41_2025,fachada_abm_2026},
while some small local models came close to the largest ones~\cite{fernandes_lorawan_2025}. Grading errors can
also harm learning, as false positives and negatives distort how students revise their work~\cite{li_am_2023}.
These observations motivate our repeated grading of every answer.

\subsection{Reasoning in Grading: An Open Question}
Reasoning, the mode in which a model writes out its thinking before it answers, is the main uncertainty. In
general LLM-as-a-judge tasks, a thinking switch within the Qwen3 family gained roughly ten accuracy points at
modest extra cost~\cite{jayarao_explicit_2025}, but the benefit depends on the task and the architecture: in
evaluating machine translation and summaries, one reasoning model improved with more reasoning while another
underperformed its own non-reasoning variant~\cite{larionov_deepseek_2025}. Reasoning traces can also run far
longer than a task needs, with verbose, redundant computation~\cite{sui_stop_2025}. Grading is a different task:
the rubric bounds what counts as correct, and the hard part is placing the score on the scale, which long
deliberation may not help. Closest to grading, Cong et al.~\cite{cong_memorization_2026} frame short-answer
grading as a move ``from memorization to reasoning'' and examine fine-tuning and rubric-based prompting. In essay
scoring, two reasoning models agreed less with expert raters, and less consistently, than a model without
reasoning~\cite{yoshida_reasoning_2025}, but that comparison sets different models against each other. Within one
model, prompted chain-of-thought (asking the model to reason step by step in its answer) improved scoring accuracy
only together with the question and the rubric~\cite{lee_cot_2024}, and higher reasoning effort raised accuracy
in mathematics scoring by amounts that varied by model family, at higher cost~\cite{frohn_reasoning_2026}. Neither
study measured how a model's built-in thinking mode affects the consistency of repeated grades, or covered code.
We switch that mode off and on within each of four models and measure agreement, consistency, and cost together.

\subsection{Rubrics and Reference Answers}
What the grader is given to grade against matters. In short-answer grading the usual material is a reference
answer, against which automatic graders have long compared the student
answer~\cite{mohler_learning_2011,dzikovska_semeval_2013}. For LLM graders, rubric-guided prompts improved
agreement with human essay scores across ten LLMs~\cite{nugroho_evaluating_2025}, a simplified rubric kept the
essay-scoring accuracy of three of four LLMs at the level of a detailed one with fewer
tokens~\cite{yoshida_detailed_2025}, and with GPT-3.5 a more explicit rubric made the grades of most of 18
engineering reports more consistent across ten runs~\cite{garcia-huertes_leveraging_2024}. Closed GPT models
scoring essays against ten predefined criteria were more consistent and agreed better with teachers than open
models, while rating more leniently than the teachers~\cite{sessler_canai_2025}. In code grading, Cohen's
$\kappa$, a chance-corrected agreement index, rose from 0.41--0.43 without a rubric to 0.60--0.65 with the best
question-specific rubric on object-oriented and data-structures code~\cite{pathak_rubric_2025}, and the best
open-source code models grading against instructor-specified rubrics reached a micro-accuracy of
85\%~\cite{havare_ai-based_2025}. Code grading with LLMs is a younger and smaller area than short-answer grading,
although locally run open-source LLMs already support teaching assistants in grading introductory
programming~\cite{mazzone_exploring_2024}, and a systematic review lists automated assessment and feedback among
the core uses of artificial intelligence in programming education~\cite{manorat_artificial_2025}.

How the model is told to apply the guidance has received less attention. A lenient and a strict prompt were
compared for two LLMs grading geography worksheets~\cite{venkatraman_llmbased_2026}, and a concurrent study found
that a lenient prompt awarding partial credit lowered the question-level grading error of every model family on a
mathematics exam~\cite{sapkota_math_2026}. Each of these studies examines one kind of task; we measure the guidance
and the prompt under one protocol, on five models and four datasets of code and short answers.

\subsection{From Model Rankings to Configuration Guidance}
A growing body of work compares LLMs as graders: side-by-side studies of programming
submissions~\cite{jukiewicz_comparison_2026} and of assignments in a bioinformatics
course~\cite{policar_automated_2025}, multi-model frameworks emphasising transparency and
reproducibility~\cite{anghel_graderassist_2025}, and benchmarks for LLM short-answer
scoring~\cite{lai_sasbench_2026}. These comparisons rank models under a fixed prompt, yet the same model's
agreement with teachers shifts with the prompt and the guidance it receives~\cite{tate_can_2024,pathak_rubric_2025},
so a ranking alone does not tell an instructor how to set up the grader. Evidence exists for single settings, such
as a simplified rubric~\cite{yoshida_detailed_2025} or a prompt that asks for partial
credit~\cite{sapkota_math_2026}, but not for the combination of settings and its cost. Our contribution is a
\emph{configuration guide}: an empirically grounded recommendation of how to configure an LLM grader for
computer-science exams, with the evidence and the cost behind each setting.
