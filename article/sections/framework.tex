The comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:results} come together in one recipe for grading with an LLM
(Table~\ref{tab:framework}, Fig.~\ref{fig:frameworkdiagram}). Give the model the question, the student
answer, and the rubric or reference answer, and ask it to award partial credit. Grade one question per
call with reasoning off and a single holistic score, in a clean conversation for every answer, with
DeepSeek-V4-Flash. Each setting is the one the comparisons favoured, and Table~\ref{tab:framework} gives the
evidence and the cost behind it.

Two settings carry most of the gain. Supplying the rubric or the reference answer was the change that
raised agreement most reliably (Section~\ref{sec:rq2}), and asking for partial credit raised it further on
code (Section~\ref{sec:rq6}). The other settings keep grading cheap and fair. Reasoning seldom helped and
always cost (Section~\ref{sec:rq1}); grading a whole exam in one call or scoring criterion by criterion never
improved agreement (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}); and a clean conversation keeps a grade independent of the
answers graded before it (Section~\ref{sec:rq5}). DeepSeek-V4-Flash is the recipe's model because, with
these settings, it graded code as well as or close to the best configuration without reasoning, at the
lowest price per grade.

\subsection{The Guide in Use}
\label{sec:guideuse}

\input{tables/tab_guide_use}

We applied the recipe to every dataset and compared it with every configuration in the study: the four
models with reasoning off and on under the strict instruction, and the partial-credit instruction on the
other models (Table~\ref{tab:guideuse}).

%% Numbers: tab_guide_use / experiments/analysis/guide_eval.py (2026-10-16, every model in).
On code, the recipe was the best configuration without reasoning on PT-CS code (0.674 QWK) and the second on
RIAYN (0.732), behind GLM-5.1 with the same instruction (0.760), which costs 15 times as much; it cost under
0.10 euros per thousand grades. On the sampled items, configurations with reasoning on, or GLM-5.1, did better
by 0.04 to 0.07, at 14 to 68 times the cost. On the public short-answer datasets, where all configurations lie
close together, the recipe stayed within 0.046 QWK of the best configuration without reasoning, which cost 19
to 53 times as much; on the 84 PT-CS short answers it was 0.123 below Claude Sonnet 4.6, at a 43rd of its
cost. Against the study's baseline configuration, Qwen3.5 with the strict instruction, the recipe raised
agreement by 0.19 on RIAYN and 0.21 on PT-CS code and matched it on short answers.

In practice the guide is a short routine: write the rubric or reference answer into the prompt, ask for
partial credit, and send each answer on its own, with reasoning off, to an inexpensive model that grades
soundly.
