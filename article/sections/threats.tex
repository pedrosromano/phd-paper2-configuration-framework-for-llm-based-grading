\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

Every setting of the guide except scope and conversation state is supported on at least one of the three
public datasets, whose human grades are independent of the authors. Scope and the conversation sub-study rest on
PT-CS alone, the only source of complete exams, and its exams are short, so the scope result holds for exams of a
few questions. The PT-CS grades were set jointly by two of the authors when they taught the course, before the
study was designed: no agreement ceiling between teachers can be computed on them, but they could not be shaped
by its results.

Four checks of the statistics, given in the supplementary material, move a few counts but change no
recommendation. Answers to the same question may be alike, and resampling whole questions instead of answers
leaves slightly fewer significant results: reasoning helps in four combinations instead of five and guidance in eleven of fifteen instead of thirteen.
The guide still agrees better than the baseline in 10 of 22 comparisons, but the baseline then agrees better once
(Claude Sonnet 4.6 on the PT-CS short answers, by 0.033), and reasoning still beats the guide in four of 17. On single runs instead of the mean of the repeated grades, the guide's QWK is about 0.01
lower. Leaving out the reasoning runs that reached the output limit makes Qwen3.5's gain on SemEval not
significant. Other rounding rules or numbers of levels make reasoning significant in six or seven combinations
instead of five; Qwen3.5 on Mohler, RIAYN, and PT-CS code and GPT-5.1 on RIAYN are significant under every rule.

The results depend on the providers that served the models, which differ in quantisation (the numeric precision
at which a model is run), inference software, and hardware, so what carries over to another provider is the
comparison between configurations rather than the absolute scores. The prompts of the follow-up experiment were
written after the zero-collapse had been found, and other prompts may do better or worse. The code prompt was
developed on two of the study's code sets with two models, and which prompt the guide uses for each type of answer
was decided after seeing the results. On rubric-graded
sets the partial-credit prompt also drops the request to grade strictly, so the two changes are measured
together.

The guide was built on four datasets and validated on two more, with the same five models; with other models
and data its standing may differ. The code evidence rests on two Java-centric datasets and one C++ dataset with
only three questions, so we draw the code conclusions more cautiously. Mohler, SemEval, and SAF have been public
for years and may be in the models' training data~\cite{sainz_contamination_2023}, although the comparisons
between configurations also hold on PT-CS, which is not public. Prompts in Portuguese might grade the Portuguese
answers differently. The expensive factors were varied one at a time, so interactions between them, such as
reasoning with decomposition, are unmeasured.
